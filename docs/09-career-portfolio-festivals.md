# 09 · Career, Portfolio & Festivals

## What it is (simply)

Turning skills into work. That means: **a portfolio** that proves what you can do, **a niche** people remember you for, **visibility** (festivals, online), and **offers** you can sell.

## Career paths for AI filmmaking engineers

| Role | What you do | Key skills from this guide |
|---|---|---|
| AI filmmaker / director | Make original shorts, music videos, series | 01–04, 07 |
| AI video generalist for agencies | Ads, product videos, social content | 02, 05, 07 |

## Portfolio: what to build

1. **Two or three finished shorts (1–5 min)** — finished beats impressive-but-unfinished every time.
2. **A 60–90 second showreel** — best shots only, strongest first, with music.
3. **Breakdowns ("how I made it")** — keyframe → clip → final, plus your pipeline diagram. Engineers get hired from breakdowns.
4. **One spec ad** for a real-style product (fictional brand to avoid trademark issues).
5. **A GitHub repo** with your ComfyUI workflows and automation scripts — proof you're an *engineer*, not just a prompt user.
6. **A simple site** (one page is fine) with contact info, reel, three projects, and services.

Each project page should state: logline, your role, tools used, time taken, what was AI vs human.

## Festivals and competitions

- **Runway AI Film Festival (AIFF)** — an annual festival celebrating films made with AI techniques; the 2024 edition had NYC and LA screenings and over $60,000 in prizes ([aif.runwayml.com/2024](https://aif.runwayml.com/2024)). Check the site for the current edition and rules.
- **FilmFreeway** — the main platform for finding and submitting to thousands of film festivals; search "AI" for AI-specific categories ([filmfreeway.com](https://filmfreeway.com/)).
- **Curious Refuge** community challenges and showcases ([curiousrefuge.com](https://curiousrefuge.com/ai-filmmaking)).
- **Traditional short-film festivals** — many now accept AI-assisted work but require disclosure. Read every festival's AI policy before paying a fee.

**Festival strategy:** pick 5–10 festivals that fit your film's length and genre; budget submission fees; keep a spreadsheet of deadlines, fees, AI rules and results; prepare a poster, stills, a 30-second trailer, a synopsis (25/50/100 words), director's statement and AI disclosure.

## Getting paid work

**Starter offers that sell:**
- 15–30 second product or event promos for local businesses.
- Music visualisers for independent musicians.
- Pitch trailers / concept teasers for writers and producers.
- Storyboards and previs for traditional shoots.
- Workshops for schools, colleges and companies.

**Pricing basics:** quote per project, not per hour, once you can estimate. Include tool costs, revisions (e.g. two rounds), delivery formats, and **usage rights**. Use the cost-per-finished-second figure from your pipeline report to stay profitable.

**Where clients come from:** your own network first, then freelance platforms, LinkedIn posts with breakdowns, short-form video showing before/after, and collaborations with editors and agencies.

## Hands-on exercise: "Portfolio sprint"

In two weeks: (1) cut a 60-second reel from your best shots, (2) write two project pages with breakdowns, (3) publish a one-page site, (4) send it to five people for feedback, (5) submit one film to one festival or challenge.

**Done when:** a stranger can understand in 30 seconds what you do and how to hire you.

## How AI helps at this stage

- Draft synopses, director's statements, cold emails and proposals (then make them sound like you).
- Generate poster concepts and thumbnail variations.
- Build your site quickly with AI coding help.
- Analyse festival rules: paste the rules and ask "what are the AI disclosure requirements, length limits and deadlines?"
